# Fly-Brain Simulation: Initial Experiments

## Objective

Explore the `flybrain` simulation, understand how selected neuron groups respond to stimulation, and investigate whether neural activity could eventually be used to generate movement commands for a fruit-fly-inspired drone.

## Experiment 1 — Load the brain model

**Purpose:** Confirm that the fly-brain model loads successfully.

**Procedure:** Loaded the model using `FlyBrain(device="auto")`.

**Results:**
- 166,700 neurons
- 25,582,938 connections
- Running on CPU

**Conclusion:** The model loaded successfully on the MacBook. GPU acceleration was not required for these initial experiments.

## Experiment 2 — Explore neuron categories

**Purpose:** Understand the types of neurons represented in the model.

**Procedure:** Inspected the counts of different neuron categories.

**Results:** The largest categories included:
- `ol_intrinsic`: 89,403
- `cb_intrinsic`: 32,164
- `vnc_intrinsic`: 13,161
- `visual_projection`: 9,201
- `descending_neuron`: 1,314
- `vnc_motor`: 708

**Conclusion:** The model contains multiple neuron groups involved in sensory processing, communication between brain regions, and movement-related pathways.

## Experiment 3 — Stimulate left looming detectors

**Purpose:** Investigate neural activity after simulating detection of an approaching object on the left.

**Procedure:** Stimulated 165 neurons associated with left-side looming detection and observed the activity of the left and right DNp01 neurons over time.

**Results:** A spike plot displayed firing events across the simulation period. The observed spike patterns differed between the two sides.

**Conclusion:** The experiment demonstrated how neural firing can be visualized over time. Further controlled tests are needed to establish how consistently the left and right DNp01 neurons respond to the stimulus.

## Experiment 4 — Visualize activity in the network

**Purpose:** Explore the spatial distribution of neurons associated with the left looming-detector stimulus.

**Procedure:** Viewed a 3D-looking visualization generated by the notebook.

**Results:** The visualization reported 4,267 neurons driven by the left looming detectors and displayed a grey cloud with red dots.

**Conclusion:** The notebook provided a spatial view of the network associated with the stimulus. The exact meaning of the highlighted neurons depends on the visualization's implementation; the count alone does not establish that all 4,267 neurons fired.

## Experiment 5 — Compare baseline and chase-related activity

**Purpose:** Examine how left LC10a stimulation relates to the firing rates of left and right DNa02 neurons.

**Procedure:** Compared the baseline (`rest`) condition with the stimulated (`chase`) condition.

**Results:**

| Neuron | Rest (spikes/s) | Chase (spikes/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Left DNa02 | 1.1 | 4.5 |
| Right DNa02 | 0.2 | 0.1 |

**Conclusion:** In this demonstration, left DNa02 activity increased substantially during the chase condition, while right DNa02 activity remained low. This suggests a side-specific response in this particular experiment.

## Experiment 6 — Count LC10a and DNa02 neurons

**Purpose:** Identify the neuron indices to use in a custom left-versus-right stimulation experiment.

**Procedure:** Used `brain.cells()` to find left and right LC10a and DNa02 neurons.

**Results:**

| Neuron group | Number found |
|---|---:|
| Left LC10a | 135 |
| Right LC10a | 140 |
| Left DNa02 | 1 |
| Right DNa02 | 1 |

**Conclusion:** The model returned neuron indices for both input groups and both monitored output neurons.

## Experiment 7 — Custom left-versus-right stimulation

**Purpose:** Test whether stimulating different sides produces distinguishable DNa02 output activity.

**Procedure:**
- Reset the brain using seed 42.
- Stimulated either the left or right LC10a neurons with an input amount of 0.8.
- Advanced the simulation for 100 steps, equivalent to 2 seconds.
- Counted recorded spikes in each DNa02 neuron.
- Divided spike counts by the simulation duration to estimate firing rates.

**Results:**

| Stimulus | Left DNa02 (spikes/s) | Right DNa02 (spikes/s) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Left LC10a | 0.0 | 1.0 |
| Right LC10a | 0.5 | 0.5 |

**Conclusion:** The custom experiment did not produce a clear, opposite left/right response. The low spike counts and differences from the quickstart setup mean that the experiment should be refined before drawing conclusions about directional control.

## Overall findings

The initial experiments established that the fly-brain model can be loaded, selected neuron groups can be stimulated, and neural activity can be visualized and measured.

The quickstart chase experiment showed a stronger left DNa02 response under its left LC10a stimulation condition. However, the custom left-versus-right experiment did not reproduce a clear directional pattern.

## Next steps

1. Inspect the original quickstart code that generates the `rest` and `chase` firing-rate comparison.
2. Reproduce its stimulation and recording procedure before modifying it.
3. Compare left and right stimulation under consistent conditions, including baseline activity and repeated trials.
4. Determine whether the output differences are reproducible.
5. If a reliable signal is found, investigate how to map it to a high-level drone command while retaining a conventional flight controller for stabilization.
